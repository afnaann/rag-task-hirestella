## Work Style and Collaboration

### How does Alex prefer to work?

Alex prefers working in small, autonomous teams where engineers have end-to-end ownership of systems. Alex is comfortable leading technically — designing architectures, reviewing code, and setting standards — while remaining an individual contributor. Alex is not looking to move into full people management at this stage.

Alex is direct in technical discussions and prefers written communication (RFCs, design documents) for decisions that affect the wider team, with synchronous meetings reserved for discussions that benefit from real-time back-and-forth.

### How does Alex approach code quality?

Alex believes that code that cannot be debugged by the engineer who wrote it six months later is not production-ready. This means Alex prioritises:
- Clear naming and structure over clever abstractions
- Explicit error handling over defensive catch-alls
- Testable modules over tightly coupled systems
- Documentation that explains *why*, not just *what*

Alex is not dogmatic about methodology (TDD, BDD, etc.) but does require meaningful test coverage for production systems.

### What has been Alex's experience with technical interviews?

Alex has conducted over 80 technical interviews as an interviewer at Luminary Labs, across ML engineering, backend engineering, and data engineering roles. This experience has shaped Alex's own approach to interviews: Alex prefers structured conversations about real projects and decisions over abstract puzzle-solving.
